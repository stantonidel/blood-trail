Filter out look-alikes
The woods are full of red things that aren't blood. These filters use shape and color rules to ignore common false positives:
  • Leaves — large, flat red patches like maple or sumac
  • Berries — small round clusters of similar-sized dots
  • Orange — bright orange tones like autumn foliage
Filtered items show with a yellow dashed outline so you can double-check.
Exclude a color
Tap a cyan area you know isn't blood. That exact color gets ignored.

    Tracking tips

    • Cyan highlights mean the color matches blood. Red sumac, dogwood and maple leaves can also trigger it — check what's highlighted before you mark it.
    • Bright red with bubbles → lung hit. Dark red → liver. Green or brown flecks → gut shot — back out and wait at least 6 hours.
    • Use Dried mode for sign that has turned brown. Raise sensitivity in low light.
    • Tap the viewfinder to freeze a frame and look closer.
    • Check the undersides of leaves and grass from knee to waist height.

    Moon—
    Sunrise and sunset are calculated from your GPS coordinates using the NOAA solar algorithm — accurate to within a minute. First light and dark are civil twilight (sun 6° below the horizon). All times use your phone's time zone. Check your state's regulations for legal shooting hours — these vary by state. Moon phase affects deer movement — a bright full moon often means more nighttime feeding and slower mornings.

    Walk the line

    Stand on each corner pin of your property and drop a point. Three or more corners make a boundary.

    Walk to each corner of your property with GPS on. At each corner, tap Drop corner here to record that point. After 3+ corners, tap Finish to close the boundary. Phone GPS is usually accurate to 5–15 yards under trees, so treat anything near the line as "on the line."

    Import boundary

    Load a KML, GPX or GeoJSON file of your property boundary.

    Remove the saved boundary?
    How to get your property lines:
    1. Open Google Earth (web or desktop) and find your land
    2. Use Add path or polygon and click around your lines — most county parcel viewers show the boundaries so you can trace them
    3. Save as a KML file, then import it here
    Never cross onto a neighbor's land to recover a deer without their permission.
